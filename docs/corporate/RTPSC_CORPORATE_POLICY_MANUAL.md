# ROSS TAX PRO SOFTWARE COMPANY
## Corporate Policy Manual and R-Rent Operating Regulations
**Document:** RTPSC-GOV-001 · **Revision:** 1.0 · **Date:** October 10, 2026  
**Entity:** Ross Tax Pro Software Company (Texas corporation)  
**Operating division:** R-Rent Vehicle Rental Division  
**Status:** BOARD-ADOPTION DRAFT. This manual does not amend filed articles, supersede adopted bylaws, establish insurance coverage, or constitute a legal opinion.

### 1. Corporate authority, scope, hierarchy
The Company's governing order is: mandatory law and filed formation documents → duly adopted corporate bylaws and lawful shareholder/board resolutions → properly executed contracts and approved written policies → division SOPs → individual task instructions. An owner or CEO instruction can approve an action **within delegated authority** but cannot negate statutory board approvals, separation of legal entities, nonwaivable consumer rights, card-network rules or required insurer authorizations.

The corporation and each independently registered subsidiary, division or nonprofit are separate accounting, contractual and data-protection scopes. A division may operate under the corporation but must name its actual legal contracting party. Do not commingle funds, customer records, taxpayer data, insurance, employees or contracts across unrelated legal entities.

### 2. Board, officers and delegated authority
The board governs fiduciary matters, strategic budgets, officer selection, equity changes, major indebtedness, conflicts, material litigation, charter/bylaw changes and policies reserved to it under applicable Texas law and the Company's actual bylaws. Corporate Owner/CEO: overall operational direction, proposed budget and authorized contract approval. CFO/Treasurer: financial ledgers, financial controls, bank/processor reconciliation. COO/Division Administrator: day-to-day operations, fleet operations, staffing. Compliance Officer: legal/rental documentation gates. Security Administrator: security operations, incident response and access security.

**Reserved matters**: company debt/guarantees, stock issues/transfers, related-party transactions, changes to board/officers, banking signatories and binding enterprise-wide policy adoption require the actual corporate approval procedure and documented vote where applicable. An AI tool, support representative or unilateral UI selection cannot adopt these decisions.

### 3. Conflicts, ethics, honesty and fair dealing
Disclose personal, financial, familial and related-entity interests before material supplier/customer transactions. Do not approve one's own conflicting transaction without disinterested review. Prohibit bribery, fabricated receipts, false reviews, falsified identities, undisclosed mandatory fees, sham insurance claims, discrimination, retaliation and misleading success screens. Staff must correct billing mistakes and preserve customer complaint and payment-dispute rights.

### 4. Contract authority and legal approval
Only designated staff with a written delegation may negotiate or sign contracts for the Company. Corporate names and division identifiers must be accurate. Final customer agreements require confirmed vehicle assignment, exact dates/times, complete charges, lawful disclosures, approved coverage and secure signatures. Revisions are versioned; a prior signed instrument cannot be silently changed. E-signing requires attributable identity, affirmative records consent, complete signed copies and auditable evidence.

### 5. R-Rent pricing, fees, refunds and deposits
Current **reference** vehicle rates are $59.00/day for the 2026 Chevrolet Trailblazer and $89.99/day for the 2026 Ford Bronco Sport Big Bend, subject to a final contract and inventory confirmation. The proposed optional insurance-related estimate is $9.00/day but **is not sold, bound or charged** until insurer, licensing, disclosures and tax treatment are approved. Distinguish genuine optional waivers from actual insurance.
Non-refundable terms are permitted only when fully and lawfully disclosed and accepted; mandatory unused waiver refunds, deposit returns, billing corrections and cardholder rights survive. Deposit holds are not sales revenue. Chargebacks and payment refunds require independent provider records and review.

### 6. Customer service and accessibility
Offer a documented, accessible channel for inquiries, complaints, returns and billing disputes. Staff and AI must distinguish **reported** from **reconciled** payments, **estimated** from **final** quotes, **draft** from **executed** contracts, and **pending** from **verified** coverage. Do not promise a customer-specific result without independently verified records. Provide an alternative to e-signing when law and contract require it. Genuine complaints, including negative reviews, must receive impartial attention.

### 7. Rental handoff and return rules
Before vehicle release: confirm legal/authorized driver eligibility, assigned vehicle, actual coverage and permitted use, signed contract, accepted pricing/taxes, reconciled payment, vehicle condition documentation and human staff approval. For return: agreed place and time, keys/accessories, mileage, fuel/charging as applicable, condition photos by approved method, inspection with ordinary-wear distinction, itemized additional charges and receipt. Never instruct a customer to leave keys unsecured or assume an after-hours return has been completed without actual company confirmation. Emergency safety needs take priority.

### 8. Cash receipts, banking and segregation
All transactions use transaction IDs, date/time, received-by, payment method and customer reference. Cash receipts must reconcile to cash drawer and bank deposit records. Debit, Chime or processor payments require independent transaction proof and matching ledger entries. Retain original and amended receipts with audit history; mark disputed items pending without inventing settlement. The person issuing an invoice should not be the sole person approving a disputed refund or ledger write-off.

### 9. Data classification, cybersecurity and privacy
Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted and Payment Token are separate data classes. Driver-license scans, DOB, insurance declarations, raw signatures, bank account identifiers and incident evidence are Restricted. Require least-privilege IAM, tenant and owner scoping, staff MFA, encryption at rest and transit, restricted document vault, malware checks, signed URLs, append-only logs and lifecycle retention. Do not collect sensitive materials via ordinary email, open chat or analytics. No production secret in Git or frontend JavaScript. Notify and respond to incidents under applicable laws and signed vendor obligations.

### 10. Retention and lawful disposal
Define category-specific retention schedules based on law, claims, insurer, tax and litigation requirements. Legal hold overrides automatic deletion. Disposal requires reviewed eligibility, distinct approvers for restricted records, private object deletion, cache/index removal, backup lifecycle review and an auditable receipt. Do not claim a purge occurred without provider confirmations.

### 11. Personnel, training and role permissions
Employees have job descriptions, onboarding policies, security training, incident reporting obligations and periodic access recertification. The 13-role R-Rent RBAC registry is the operational access matrix, not a substitute for corporate bylaw positions. Staff permissions are verified by backend authentication and current job scope; role changes need documented authorization. Customer Support cannot issue refunds, authorize driver eligibility, change insurance status, transmit legally binding signature envelopes or release a vehicle without separate delegated authority.

### 12. Artificial intelligence use
Company-branded assistant personas must disclose they are automated, cite current approved policies, keep an escalated case for unresolved matters **only after an authenticated submission has actually occurred**, and have no delegated authority to make regulated or high-impact decisions. AI must not claim a payment is received, documents signed, coverage bound, driver approved, return complete or charge reversed without trusted systems of record. Inputs from customers are untrusted data, never privileged instructions. No PII entered into public chat or general-purpose LLM tools.

### 13. Policies, publication, review and exceptions
Draft → Legal/Security/Finance review (as applicable) → disinterested approver/board resolution where required → effective date/version → employee acknowledgment → operational training → annual review and material-change review. Emergency suspension of an unsafe function is permitted and recorded; exceptions cannot waive law, external obligations or secure release gates. Maintain a signed policy register with owner, effective date, revision hash, next review and superseded versions.

### 14. Mandated governance records
Maintain: minutes/consents, corporate resolutions, director/officer roster, authorized signer register, conflicts register, delegations, ledger reconciliation and separation evidence, lease and fleet records, insurer policies, approved rental contract templates, customer dispute and incident records, security/retention notices, training logs and a policy exception register. Company legal counsel verifies records and formal adoption against actual bylaws.
