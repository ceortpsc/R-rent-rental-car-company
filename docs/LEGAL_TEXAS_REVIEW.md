# Texas Legal and Regulatory Reference — review required

This document is background for qualified Texas counsel, not a legal opinion or a substitute for an authorized insurance/agent agreement.

Conventional rentals: Texas Business & Commerce Code Chapter 91 (private passenger vehicle rental companies). The damage waiver requires written consent at/before signature (§91.051), mandatory waiver notice in at least 10-point type (§91.052), site notice (§91.053), no coercion after refusal (§91.054), all unavoidable mandatory charges displayed clearly in all advertised prices and contracts (§91.055), narrowly defined valid waiver voiding terms (§91.056), and refunds for qualifying ineffective/excess waiver periods (§91.057, amended 2025). Do not preselect a damage waiver or call it "insurance".

Texas Comptroller: state rental gross-receipts tax is 10% for contracts 1–30 days and 6.25% for 31–180 days. Certain separately stated insurance, fuel and damage charges may be excluded. City/county rental taxes can apply. Rates must be assessed under actual contract and location before final settlement.

Electronic agreements: Texas Business & Commerce Code Chapter 322 and federal E-SIGN Act; actual customer consent to electronic records, delivery/access and version-preserving audit evidence are required.

Future vehicle-owner host marketplace: Texas Business & Commerce Code Chapter 113 imposes distinct primary insurance, disclosure, eligible driver, record retention and claims requirements. Do not activate third-party host listings on conventional rental coverage without independent review.
